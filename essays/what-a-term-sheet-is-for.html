What a Term Sheet Is For

Corporate Finance · November 2026

This is placeholder text. It shows how a finished essay will look on the page, including a subheading, a quotation and notes.

A term sheet is usually the first document that the parties to a financing put in writing, and it is usually the shortest one that they will sign. It records the amount, the price, the tenor and a handful of conditions, and it leaves almost everything else to the long-form agreements that follow.1

Lawyers sometimes treat the document as a formality because most of its provisions are expressed to be non-binding. That view overlooks how the document is used in practice, since the commercial points settled at this stage are rarely reopened once the drafting of the definitive agreements has begun.

What the parties are agreeing to

The provisions that do bind are few, and they tend to concern confidentiality, exclusivity, costs and governing law. Each of them protects the time and money that the parties are about to spend on due diligence and documentation, which is why they are carved out from the general statement that the term sheet creates no obligation to close.

The remaining provisions work differently. They do not oblige anyone to lend or to borrow, but they set the reference point against which every later draft will be compared, and a party that departs from them will be asked to explain why.2

Why brevity is deliberate

A short term sheet lets the principals read and decide on the whole of the bargain in one sitting, without relying on a summary prepared by someone else. The detail that is left out is detail that the principals have chosen to delegate, and the long-form agreements are where that delegated work is done.
